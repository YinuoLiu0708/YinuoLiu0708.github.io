Evans S.W., Bhaskar S.K., <u>Liu Y.</u>, Youngstrom E.A., Owens J.S., & Rosenthal E.A.

**Status**: Under Review at *Pediatrics*

**Abstract**

**Objective:** To provide national norms, percentiles, and results of factor analyses for the ADHD symptoms and performance items on the parent-report and self-report versions of the Vanderbilt Assessment Scale (VAS) for youth ages 6 to 17. 

**Method:** A nationally stratified survey sample of 2,230 parents and the adolescent children (aged 12-17) of 996 of the parents completed the parent and adolescent self-report versions. Both versions contained 18 ADHD symptoms and 8 performance items. To evaluate the psychometric and age-related normative curves of the VAS across parent- and youth-report forms we conducted exploratory factor analysis, confirmatory factor analysis, and measurement invariance testing, and generated age and sex-specific norms.

**Results:** Factor analyses produced the expected symptom factors and two performance factors. Developmental and sex-specific patterns of ratings of inattention and hyperactivity/impulsivity symptoms indicated that after increasing slightly during the preadolescent years, parent ratings of inattention symptoms decline and remain stable. In contrast, ratings of hyperactivity/impulsivity decline steadily with age. Parent ratings of males were higher than females on both factors although sex differences were smaller with self-ratings. Generating the normative data using GAMLSS estimates centiles from fitted probability distributions reduced sampling variability and produced stable normative curves rather than empirical percentile estimations within discrete age bands.

**Conclusion and Policy Implications:** This study provides norms for the parent and adolescent self-report VAS. The findings underscore the importance of using sex and age-based norms for severity interpretations and diagnostic decision making. A quick reference guide for practitioners is provided in Supplementary materials.
